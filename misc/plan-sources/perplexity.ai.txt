Your approach is solid, but I’d tighten the normalization rules before hardcoding more mappings. The biggest improvements are: preserve recognizable public brand suffixes like “.com”, “.ai”, “.io”, and “.fm” when they are part of the brand, and treat legal suffixes like Ltd/Inc/LLC as removable noise rather than part of the display name. [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a553289)

## Places to refine
- Some of your mappings mix brand names with organizational aliases, so I’d split them into two layers: a canonical company name and an optional “display alias.” For example, “MalamTeam” vs “MALAM,” “hms” vs “HMS,” and “PAPAYA” vs “Papaya Global” are better handled as one canonical label with case-normalized variants. [developers.google](https://developers.google.com/people/v1/contacts)
- A few entries look like they should keep punctuation or dots because that is the recognizable brand, such as SVT.Jobs, J.P. Morgan, eBay, AT&T, Wix.com, and GlassesUSA.com. [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a423102/requirements-for-company-targeting-lists-in-campaign-manager?lang=en-us&intendedLocale=en)

## Rules I’d add
- Remove company legal endings unless they are the brand itself: Ltd, Limited, Inc, LLC, Corp, Co, Holdings, Group, Technologies, Systems, International, Israel.
- Keep suffixes that are part of the brand: .com, .ai, .io, .fm, J.P., AT&T, eBay, SVT.Jobs, GlassesUSA.com. [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a553289)
- Normalize only after deciding whether the name is a parent company, subsidiary, or brand page; LinkedIn company names can change and can differ from URLs, so the page name is not always the best canonical key. [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a566295)
- Use a manual exception list for government, military, and university pages, because those often have descriptive variants that should not be over-shortened. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_companies_of_Israel)

## Suggested cleanup logic
1. Normalize spacing and punctuation.
2. Remove generic suffixes.
3. Apply your exception dictionary.
4. Collapse known variants to one canonical name.
5. Keep the original raw company name as a backup field for traceability.

A practical example would be: “Rafael Advanced Defense Systems Ltd.” → strip legal suffix, match exception, store `Rafael` as canonical and `Rafael Advanced Defense Systems` as original source. [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a566295)

## Extra note
Your list is already strong enough to start, but I would avoid forcing every long name into a short one if it risks collisions, because multiple distinct companies can share the same umbrella brand or similar employer page names. The safest rule is: shorten only when the shorter name is still uniquely recognizable to you and your future search workflow.
